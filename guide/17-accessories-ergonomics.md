# Chapter 17 — Accessories and Ergonomics: What's Actually Worth Buying

A laptop is a compromise: the screen is at the wrong height, the keyboard is attached to the screen, and the ports are few. The right $200–600 of accessories fixes more of your daily experience than a $500 laptop upgrade. In rough order of value per dollar:

## 17.1 External monitor — the highest-impact purchase

- **27" 4K IPS (3840×2160) at 150% scaling** — the developer standard. Sharp text, room for two editors side by side, one cable. **$250–450**: Dell S2722QC / U2723QE (USB-C with 65–90 W PD — the monitor becomes your dock), LG 27UP850/27US500, BenQ PD2706U, Asus ProArt PA279CRV, Samsung ViewFinity S8/S9. Look for USB-C with ≥65 W power delivery, a built-in USB hub, and height adjustment.
- **27" 1440p IPS (2560×1440) at 100%** — cheaper ($180–300), less sharp, still good. Fine on Windows/Linux; macOS renders 1440p at 100% a bit soft (no HiDPI).
- **32" 4K** — a lot of space at 125–150%; needs a deep desk.
- **34" ultrawide 3440×1440** — great for side-by-side; text less sharp than 4K; awkward for video calls and screen sharing.
- **Two 27" 4K** — the professional's setup; needs TB4/USB4 or a dock; MacBook Air supports exactly two.
- **5K 27" (Apple Studio Display $1,599; Samsung ViewFinity S9; Asus ProArt PA27JCV ~$800)** — 218 PPI, perfect macOS 2× scaling; luxurious, not necessary.
- **Portable monitor (15–16" 1080p/2.5K, USB-C, $120–300)** — a second screen in the dorm or library; the Asus ZenScreen and Arzopa lines are fine. Genuinely useful for students who move.

## 17.2 Laptop stand + external keyboard + mouse — the ergonomics trio

- **Stand ($20–60):** raises the screen to eye level (top of screen ≈ eye height). Rain mStand, Twelve South Curve, Nexstand (folding, for travel), or a $20 aluminum riser. Also improves cooling.
- **Keyboard ($60–180):** once the laptop is raised, you need one. Low-profile: Logitech MX Keys S / MX Mechanical Mini, Keychron K3/K7, NuPhy Air75. Full mechanical: Keychron Q/V series, Ducky, GMMK. Ergonomic/split (worth it if you have any wrist pain): ZSA Voyager/Moonlander, Kinesis Advantage 360, Keychron Q11, Dygma. Mac users: Apple Magic Keyboard with Touch ID (Touch ID works on Apple Silicon Macs — genuinely convenient).
- **Mouse/trackpad ($30–130):** Logitech MX Master 3S (multi-device, horizontal scroll — excellent for code and spreadsheets), MX Anywhere 3S (portable), Apple Magic Trackpad (gestures), or a vertical mouse (Logitech Lift/MX Vertical) for wrist issues. **Multi-device switching** (Logitech Bolt/Bluetooth, 3 devices) lets one keyboard/mouse serve laptop + desktop + tablet.

## 17.3 Dock or hub

Chapter 9 §9.4 in brief: **Thunderbolt 4/USB4 dock ($150–300)** for a real desk (CalDigit TS4/TS5, Anker 778/Prime, Plugable TBT4-UDZ, Kensington SD5780T, Lenovo/Dell/HP TB4 docks); **USB-C monitor with PD** as a poor-man's dock; **$30–60 travel hub** with HDMI 2.0 + USB-A ×2 + 100 W PD pass-through for the bag (Anker, Ugreen, Satechi). DisplayLink dock if you need a third monitor on a MacBook Air.

## 17.4 Chargers and cables

- **65–100 W GaN charger ($30–70):** Anker Prime/Nano, Ugreen Nexode, Baseus, Satechi. Two USB-C + one USB-A, 100 W total, smaller than the OEM brick. One for the desk, one for the bag.
- **A 240 W-rated USB-C cable** (marked, ~$15–25): future-proof for EPR; any 100 W cable is fine for today's ultraportables. **Thunderbolt 4 cable** (40 Gb/s, active for >1 m) if you'll connect a dock or fast SSD — plain USB-C cables often cap at USB 2.0 data.
- **MagSafe cable** (Apple) is included with MacBooks; a spare is $49.
- **Power bank with 65–100 W USB-C PD output** (Anker Prime 20K/27K, Ugreen 145 W): charges a laptop through a long day of classes. 20,000–27,000 mAh is the airline carry-on ceiling (100 Wh).

## 17.5 Bag and protection

- **Backpack with a padded, suspended laptop compartment**: Bellroy, Aer, Peak Design, Tom Bihn, Osprey, Timbuk2, or a well-reviewed $50–80 option. Compartment fit matters (14" vs 16").
- **Sleeve** for inside a non-padded bag; not needed in a good backpack.
- **Skip:** hard-shell snap-on cases for MacBooks — they trap heat, scratch the finish under the clips, and add weight. A sleeve does the job.
- **Screen protector:** no. **Keyboard cover:** no (traps heat, can mark the screen on MacBooks). **Webcam cover:** sure, if it's thin (< 0.1 mm) — thick ones crack MacBook screens when the lid closes.

## 17.6 Storage and backup

- **External NVMe SSD ($90–180 for 1–2 TB):** Samsung T7 Shield / T9, SanDisk Extreme Pro, Crucial X9/X10 Pro, or a Thunderbolt/USB4 enclosure + your own drive for 3,000 MB/s. For Time Machine / File History, project archives, media, VM images.
- **Cloud backup:** Backblaze ($9/month, unlimited) or iCloud+/Google One/OneDrive (often free via university) — the second copy, off-site.
- **USB-C flash drive (64–256 GB, ~$15–30):** for bootable installers, lab file transfers, and that one professor who insists.

## 17.7 Audio and video

- **Headphones:** for focus and calls. Any comfortable closed-back or ANC set — Sony WH-1000XM6, Bose QC Ultra, AirPods Pro 3 (Mac), Sennheiser Momentum 4 — or wired for zero-latency and no charging (Audio-Technica M50x, Sony MDR-7506, Sennheiser HD 560S). Multipoint Bluetooth matters if you switch between laptop and phone.
- **Webcam/mic upgrades** only if your laptop's are bad: Logitech Brio 4K/MX Brio, Insta360 Link 2 (auto-framing), Elgato Facecam; USB mic (Samson Q2U, Fifine, Shure MV6/MV7+) or a headset (Jabra Evolve2, EPOS) for a lot of meetings. Apple's Continuity Camera (iPhone as webcam) is excellent and free.

## 17.8 Ergonomic extras

- **Chair before desk.** Any adjustable chair with lumbar support beats an expensive desk. Secondlab/used Herman Miller/Steelcase from office liquidators are $200–400.
- **Monitor arm ($30–150):** frees desk space, dials in height. Amazon Basics/Ergotron.
- **Wrist rest:** only if your keyboard is tall.
- **Lap desk with a hard surface** for couch/bed work — protects thermals (Chapter 10) and posture.
- **Blue-light/night modes** are built into every OS. Warm the screen after dark; it doesn't help your eyes as much as it helps your sleep.
- **The 20-20-20 rule** and a standing break every hour matter more than any product here.

## 17.9 Tools and small things

- **iFixit Pro Tech / Essential toolkit ($25–75):** for SSD upgrades, fan cleaning, and battery swaps on PC laptops. Framework ships a screwdriver.
- **Compressed air** (or an electric duster) for fans once a year.
- **Microfiber cloth + screen cleaner** (no alcohol on anti-glare coatings).
- **Cable ties / a cable organizer pouch** for the bag.
- **Bluetooth tracker** in the bag (AirTag / Tile / Chipolo).
- **Laptop lock** (Kensington/Noble) if you'll leave it in a lab or library — most business laptops have a slot; MacBooks don't.

## 17.10 A sane starter kit by budget

| Budget | Buy |
|---|---|
| **$100** | 65 W GaN charger + USB-C→HDMI/USB-A hub + 128 GB flash drive + microfiber |
| **$300** | Above + 27" 1440p IPS monitor + $20 laptop stand + basic Bluetooth keyboard/mouse |
| **$600** | 27" 4K USB-C monitor with PD (doubles as dock) + stand + MX Keys/Keychron + MX Master + 1 TB external SSD + GaN charger |
| **$1,000+** | Two 27" 4K or one 5K, TB4 dock, monitor arms, mechanical or ergonomic keyboard, good chair, ANC headphones, 2 TB TB4 SSD |
