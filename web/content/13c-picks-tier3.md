## Tier 3 — $1,000–1,500: "The mainstream recommendation"

New MacBook Airs live here after the June price increase, alongside the best Windows ultraportables *on sale*.

### 🥇 Pick: Apple MacBook Air 13" / 15" M5 (2026) — $1,299 / $1,499 (edu: −$100, +$100 gift card through Sep 24, 2026)
- **What:** M5 (10-core CPU, 8- or 10-core GPU), 16 GB (24/32 optional), **512 GB base**, 13.6" 2560×1664 or 15.3" 2880×1864 500-nit IPS 60 Hz, 12 MP Center Stage, 2× TB4 + MagSafe 3, Wi-Fi 7 / BT 6, 53.8 / 66.5 Wh, 1.24 / 1.51 kg, fanless, four/six speakers.
- **Config to buy:** **13" or 15" with 24 GB / 512 GB — $1,499 / $1,699.** The 24 GB step is the best $200 you can spend. 16/512 ($1,299/$1,499) if that's the ceiling. 1 TB (+$200) for mobile devs and media hoarders.
- **Pros:** PCMag Editors' Choice, Wirecutter's top laptop overall, AppleInsider's "best buy for most." Fastest single-core of any laptop; multi-core in 55 W-Intel territory with no fan; ~12–14 h real use; best-in-class trackpad, speakers, webcam, build; macOS dev environment; 7-year support; ~50% resale at 3 years.
- **Cons:** $200 more than in May. 60 Hz IPS while $900 Windows laptops have 120 Hz OLED. Two ports, both on the left. No upgrades. Not for CUDA/Windows-only.
- **Who:** Most CS students and most software engineers who don't have a specific reason to be elsewhere. 15" if it's your only screen; 13" if you carry it everywhere and dock.

### 🥈 Pick: Lenovo ThinkPad X1 Carbon Gen 13 (Lunar Lake, 32 GB) or Gen 14 Aura Edition (Panther Lake) — $1,300–1,600 on sale
- **What (Gen 14, 2026):** Core Ultra 7/X7 Series 3, 14" 2.8K OLED 120 Hz or 1200p/2.8K IPS options, 16–64 GB LPDDR5X (soldered), 512 GB–2 TB, 57–60 Wh, **~1.0 kg** (carbon fiber), 2× TB4 + 2× USB-A + HDMI, 8 MP IR camera with shutter, optional 5G, haptic trackpad, TrackPoint, MIL-STD. Reviews: Thurrott "Panther Lake perfection"; Notebookcheck "excellent… impressive battery… pay attention to the display" (some panel options are weak — get the 2.8K OLED or the high-brightness IPS); PCMag $2,139 list.
- **Config:** Core Ultra 7 358H (X7 if offered), **32 GB**, 1 TB, 2.8K OLED (or the 500-nit IPS if PWM-sensitive). Wait for a Lenovo sale — Gen 13 routinely hit ~$1,400; Gen 14 will follow within months. Never pay $2,139.
- **Pros:** The best keyboard on any laptop; 1.0 kg; Linux-certified; real ports on a 1 kg machine; 12+ h battery on Gen 14; Premier Support.
- **Cons:** List price; soldered RAM; speakers merely decent; some display SKUs are mediocre (read the review of *your* panel); Gen 14 not yet discounted as of September 2026 — Gen 13 (Lunar Lake 258V/268V, 32 GB on-package) is the value play at ~$1,300–1,400.
- **Who:** Windows/Linux users who type all day, travel, and want the most professional ultraportable. The Mac alternative for people who won't buy a Mac.

### 🥉 Pick: Framework Laptop 13 Pro (Panther Lake, Core Ultra 5 325 / X7 358H) — $1,499–2,100 prebuilt / ~$1,350+ DIY
- **What:** 13.5" 3:2 2880×1920 IPS 30–120 Hz **700 nits, touch, matte**, Core Ultra 5 325 (base) or X7 358H, LPCAMM2 LPDDR5X 16–64 GB (**upgradeable**, but $239/16 GB, $800/32 GB in 2026), M.2 2280, **74 Wh** (Ars Technica: **18 h** PCMark @200 nits), machined 6063 aluminum, haptic trackpad, four swappable expansion cards, 1.3 kg, Ubuntu 24.04 preinstall option; Ubuntu 26.04 & Fedora 44 official; Arch/NixOS/Bazzite community. Reviews: Ars "right laptop, wrong time"; PCMag "a MacBook rival you can repair"; Tom's Hardware "cleans up nice."
- **Config:** Core Ultra 5 325 prebuilt 16 GB / 512 GB ($1,499) if budget-bound; DIY with X7 358H mainboard + 16 GB LPCAMM2 + your own 1 TB SSD + Linux ≈ $1,600–1,700; 32 GB pushes to $2,100+.
- **Pros:** 10/10 repairability; upgrade the CPU in 2029 by swapping the mainboard; best Linux story outside System76; 700-nit matte 3:2 panel is arguably the best *programming* display on any Windows/Linux laptop; 18 h battery fixes Framework's historic weakness; 100 W USB-C charging; choose your ports.
- **Cons:** Expensive for the spec because of LPCAMM2 pricing; four ports at once; speakers/webcam fine, not great; Core Ultra 5 325 has the small GPU tile — fine for dev, weak for games.
- **Who:** Linux users; people who plan to keep a laptop 6–8 years; anyone who values repairability. The original **Framework 13 (Ryzen AI 300, DDR5 SO-DIMM, 61 Wh)** is the cheaper route to 32 GB (~$1,250–1,400 DIY) with ~8.5 h battery.

### Also consider
- **Dell XPS 14 (2026, DA14260, Panther Lake X7, 32 GB, 3.2K OLED)** — $1,600–2,100 (Dell list $2,079). Tom's Hardware: "two steps forward" — physical function row returns, +55% battery vs. prior gen, excellent speakers/webcam, shallow keyboard. Dell's frequent 15–20% sales bring it into this tier.
- **HP OmniBook Ultra 14 (2026, Panther Lake or Snapdragon X2)** — $1,200–1,500. CNET's favorite Windows laptop of 2026; X2 version ~11 h real uptime (Thurrott). Good keyboard, 32 GB configs.
- **Microsoft Surface Laptop 8th Edition (13.8", Snapdragon X2 Plus/Elite, 16/512)** — $1,599 list, seen at **$1,299** on the Microsoft Store (Sep 2026). Superb build, 3:2 120 Hz 600-nit touch, best keyboard/trackpad on Windows, 15+ h. Arm caveats. Buy on sale.
- **Lenovo Yoga Slim 7x Gen 11 (Snapdragon X2 Elite, 14.5" 3K OLED)** — $1,200–1,500. Excellent battery, light, OLED. Arm caveats.
- **Asus Zenbook S14 (2026, Panther Lake, Ceraluminum, 3K OLED 120 Hz)** — $1,300–1,900 by SKU. Refined build, brighter OLED, quiet; but its Panther Lake SKU has the *small* GPU tile (no B390). 1.2 kg, 72 Wh. **Zenbook S16** (Ryzen AI 400) for 16".
- **ThinkPad T14s Gen 7 AMD (Ryzen AI 7 PRO 450, up to 64 GB)** — $1,200–1,500 on sale. Notebookcheck "excellent"; the Linux workhorse; more RAM options than X1 Carbon.
- **Apple MacBook Air M4 15" refurb, 24 GB** — $1,100–1,300. Same argument as Tier 2, bigger screen.

### Avoid in this tier
- **MacBook Pro 14" M5 base at $1,999** — over budget *and* due to be replaced by the (now-announced) M6 late this year. Wait or buy refurb.
- Thin Windows machines with 16 GB soldered at $1,300+ — insist on 32 GB at this price, or buy the Air.
- Anything with Intel Arrow Lake H or 14th-gen Core in a thin chassis.
