## Tier 2 — $700–1,000: "The value sweet spot"

This is where a refurbished MacBook Air enters and, for most students, ends the discussion.

### 🥇 Pick: Apple MacBook Air 13" M4 (2025), Certified Refurbished, 16 GB — $850–950
- **What:** M4 (10-core CPU / 8- or 10-core GPU), 16 GB, 256 GB (or 512 GB for ~$100 more), 13.6" 2560×1664 500-nit IPS, 12 MP Center Stage camera, 2× TB4 + MagSafe, 53.8 Wh, 1.24 kg, fanless, Wi-Fi 6E, quad speakers, Touch ID. Supports two external displays.
- **Where:** Apple Certified Refurbished (new battery, new shell, 1-year warranty, AppleCare-eligible; stock fluctuates), Back Market (Gizmodo reported $859 in July 2026), Amazon Renewed, Best Buy open-box.
- **Config:** 16/256 is the bargain (~$850); add a 1–2 TB external SSD ($100–150) for media and archives. 16/512 (~$950–1,000) or 24/512 if you find one under $1,050.
- **Pros:** ~85–88% of an M5 Air's speed for two-thirds the price; everything great about the Air (Chapter 3 §3.3): silence, 12+ hours, best trackpad, macOS Unix, 7 years of updates, high resale. Was $999–1,099 *new* six months ago.
- **Cons:** 256 GB fills fast (external SSD or 512 GB config); 60 Hz display; no upgrades ever; refurb stock varies week to week.
- **Who:** The default recommendation for any CS student whose budget tops out around $1,000 and who doesn't need Windows-only tools or CUDA.

### 🥈 Pick: Lenovo ThinkPad T14 Gen 6 / T14s Gen 6 (AMD Ryzen AI 7 PRO 350/360, 32 GB) — $900–1,100 on sale
- **What:** 2025 ThinkPad with Ryzen AI 300 (Zen 5), 14" 1920×1200 IPS 400-nit low-power (or 2.8K OLED option), 16–32 GB (T14: one SO-DIMM + soldered — configure 32; T14s: soldered LPDDR5X, configure 32), 512 GB–1 TB, 52.5–58 Wh, 1.2–1.3 kg (T14s), full port array on T14 (USB-A ×2, HDMI, RJ45 on some), 1080p/5 MP camera with shutter, fingerprint, IR.
- **Where:** Lenovo.com sales (weekly; also student store via ID.me/UNiDAYS), Lenovo Outlet, Costco/B&H.
- **Pros:** The keyboard; Linux-certified and flawless on Fedora/Ubuntu; upgradeable SSD (and RAM on T14); MIL-STD; Premier Support/ADP add-ons are cheap; boring reliability. Notebookcheck rated the T14s Gen 7 AMD (2026, Ryzen AI 7 PRO 450) "excellent" with 64 GB options — Gen 6 is the same chassis one step behind, cheaper.
- **Cons:** Base 1200p panel is good-not-great (get the 400-nit low-power or OLED); weak speakers; 8–10 h real battery; AMD fan noise on Zoom calls is present but modest; list prices are absurd — only buy on sale.
- **Who:** Windows or Linux students who want the most durable, repairable, port-rich machine under $1,100; EE/CE students; anyone who touches hardware.

### 🥉 Pick: Asus Zenbook 14 OLED (2025, Ryzen AI 7 350 / Core Ultra 7 258V, 16–32 GB) — $750–950 on sale
- **What:** 14" 3K OLED 120 Hz (or 1200p OLED 60 Hz on cheaper SKUs — check), 16 or 32 GB soldered, 1 TB, 75 Wh, 1.2 kg, USB4 ×2 + USB-A + HDMI, Harman Kardon speakers, 1080p IR camera.
- **Pros:** OLED and 75 Wh at this price; light; Lunar Lake version has superb battery (12+ h); AMD version has better multi-core; good ports for a thin machine.
- **Cons:** Shallow keyboard; Asus mail-in support; OLED PWM; check exact SKU (Asus has dozens with wildly different panels/RAM).
- **Who:** Students who want an OLED Windows ultraportable with long battery and don't need ThinkPad ruggedness.

### Also consider
- **Framework Laptop 13 (Ryzen AI 300, DIY Edition)** — ~$900 barebones + your own DDR5 SO-DIMMs (32 GB ~$250–300 in 2026) + SSD ($80–120) + Linux ≈ $1,250–1,350 all-in. Slightly over tier, but the only fully repairable/upgradeable pick; 61 Wh ≈ 8.5 h; the 3:2 2880×1920 120 Hz display is excellent.
- **HP OmniBook Ultra 14 (2025, Ryzen AI 9 HX 375, 32 GB)** — $900–1,100 on sale. 32 GB standard is the draw; 2.2K IPS.
- **Microsoft Surface Laptop 7 (13.8", Snapdragon X Elite, 16 GB)** — $800–950 on sale (2024 model, superseded by the 8th Edition). Superb build, 3:2 120 Hz touch, great keyboard/trackpad, 12+ h. Windows-on-Arm caveats.
- **Lenovo Yoga Slim 7i Aura (Lunar Lake 258V, 32 GB)** — $850–1,000 on sale; 32 GB on-package, OLED, long battery.
- **MacBook Air 13" M2/M3 refurb, 16 GB** — $650–800. M3 is a fine chip; M2 acceptable; **only with 16 GB**. An M3 16/512 under $800 is a great buy.
- **Used MacBook Pro 14" M1 Pro (2021), 16/512** — $700–900 used. Mini-LED 120 Hz, HDMI, SD, MagSafe, great speakers; M1 Pro is still roughly M4-class in multi-core. Check cycle count < 400. 1.6 kg. A sleeper pick for the Pro display and ports on a budget.

### Avoid in this tier
- New Windows laptops with 16 GB soldered and no upgrade path at $900+ — you can do better.
- **Gaming laptops under $1,000 as a daily driver** (LOQ/TUF/Victus with RTX 5050) — only if you *need* a GPU (Chapter 12).
- Thin machines with Intel Arrow Lake H (Core Ultra 7 255H) — fast, but hot, loud, and 5–7 h battery; Lunar Lake or Ryzen AI are better here.
